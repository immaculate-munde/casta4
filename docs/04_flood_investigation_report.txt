INDEPENDENT FLOOD INVESTIGATION REPORT

Prepared by: Ridgeway Loss Adjusters & Assessors Ltd
Report Reference: RWA/FLD/2026/0412
For: Amani General Insurance Company Limited
Re: Kariobangi Cold Storage & Logistics Limited - Plot 7, Outer Ring Road,
    Kariobangi South, Nairobi
Claim Reference: AGI/CLM/2026/2048
Date of Report: 28 April 2026
Date of Site Inspection: 18 April 2026

1. INSTRUCTIONS

We were instructed by Amani General Insurance Company Limited to attend the site,
investigate the cause and mechanism of the flood loss of 14–15 April 2026, and to
comment on the extent of loss and the condition of flood-mitigation measures
(drainage clearance and elevated storage / flood mark) at the time of the incident.

2. FINDINGS - CAUSE AND MECHANISM

2.1 Physical evidence and neighbour statements confirm that intense overnight
    rainfall produced widespread surface-water ponding along Outer Ring Road and
    the Kariobangi South drainage corridor on 14–15 April 2026. This is consistent
    with known Eastlands pluvial hotspots (Kariobangi, Dandora, and adjacent
    catchments) used in Kenya Re's Nairobi flood desk hotspot list.
2.2 Water entered the Insured's yard primarily from the roadside storm-water
    channel adjacent to the loading bay. At inspection we found the channel
    heavily silted, with solid waste and packaging debris obstructing the culvert
    inlet serving the site frontage. Water marks on the loading-bay walls indicate
    peak external depth of approximately 50–55 cm above yard level.
2.3 Inside the main cold-room hall, water marks and silt lines indicate peak
    inundation of approximately 38 cm above finished floor level — above the
    Policy's declared flood mark of 0.45 m only in the sense that stock on the
    lowest tier (stored at roughly 0.15–0.20 m above floor on pallets) was fully
    immersed; the declared warranty requires lowest storage not less than 0.45 m.
2.4 In our professional opinion, the most probable cause is accidental pluvial /
    urban flood arising from extreme rainfall combined with inadequate local
    drainage conveyance at the site frontage. We found no evidence of deliberate
    diversion of water onto the premises by a third party. We cannot entirely
    exclude contribution from upstream public-system surcharge, but site-level
    blockage materially increased the depth and duration of inundation on this risk.

3. FINDINGS - FLOOD MITIGATION AND WARRANTIES

3.1 Drainage clearance (Warranty 1): We requested Q1 2026 drainage clearance /
    maintenance logs required under Warranty 1(a). At inspection, the Insured
    produced only an informal WhatsApp instruction to a casual labourer dated
    February 2026; no dated photographic log or contractor invoice for the
    mandatory 90-day clearance cycle covering March–April 2026 was available.
    The obstructed culvert inlet and silted channel are, in our view, consistent
    with lack of recent clearance and are more likely than not to have increased
    the depth of water entering the loading bay.
3.2 Temporary flood defences (Warranty 1(b)): A small store of sandbags was present
    but partially degraded and not pre-positioned at the loading-bay doors when
    the Kenya Meteorological Department heavy-rainfall advisory for Nairobi was
    issued on the afternoon of 14 April 2026. Staff placed bags after water was
    already rising — too late to prevent entry.
3.3 Elevated storage / flood mark (Warranty 2): Ground-tier pallets of frozen
    foodstuffs were stored with base height approximately 0.15–0.20 m above floor
    level, below the 0.45 m declared flood mark. Upper racking above ~0.50 m was
    largely dry. Stock loss is concentrated on the non-compliant lower tiers.
    Plant motors mounted at floor level in the plant room were similarly below
    any elevated mounting that might have limited electrical damage.
3.4 We are not qualified to opine on the legal effect of any warranty breach; that
    is a matter for the Insurer's claims and legal assessment. We can confirm that
    the drainage and storage deficiencies noted above are, in our professional
    opinion, more likely than not to have contributed to the extent of the loss.

plant salvage assumptions, pending final reconciliation.

5. OBSERVATIONS RELEVANT TO POLICY WARRANTIES

We note for the Insurer's consideration that the apparent lapse in drainage
clearance (Warranty 1) and storage below the declared flood mark (Warranty 2)
may be relevant to warranty compliance and to any reduction of indemnity. The
loss itself is assessed as a genuine flood event; the open questions are extent
and warranty application, not whether flooding occurred.

6. RECOMMENDATIONS

  1. Obtain contractor invoices / dated photographic logs for drainage clearance
     covering January–April 2026, or confirm their absence.
  2. Finalise stock write-off against the 13 April inventory and temperature logs.
  3. Obtain asset-register / invoice support for refrigeration plant claimed.
  4. Consider Kenya Re referral under Treaty Article 6 given gross loss above
     KES 60,000,000, outstanding documentation, and warranty doubt.
  5. We consider the claim, subject to the above outstanding items and the
     Insurer's assessment of warranty compliance, to be a genuine pluvial flood
     loss arising from the April 2026 Eastlands rainfall event.
